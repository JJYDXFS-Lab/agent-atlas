title: Design and organizational decision-making
---

How do systems pursue goals with limited resources, and how do organizations shape their choices? This route begins with selected concepts from Herbert A. Simon's _The Sciences of the Artificial_ (third edition, 1996) and _Administrative Behavior_ (fourth edition, 1997). Continue to [[collaboration-across-people-and-artefacts|Collaboration across people and artefacts]] for distinct accounts of distributed work, situated interaction, and joint control.

## Design under constraints

- [[artifact-interface|Artifact interface]] — the relation among a system's organization, environment, and purpose.
- [[bounded-rationality|Bounded rationality]] — limits on knowledge, anticipation, attention, and alternatives considered.
- [[satisficing|Satisficing]] — searching for an alternative that meets stated standards of acceptability.
- [[means-ends-analysis|Means–ends analysis]] — selecting actions by relating state differences to relevant operators.
- [[near-decomposability|Near decomposability]] — stronger within-subsystem interactions and weaker, slower cross-subsystem effects.

[[cognitive-requirements|Cognitive requirements]] connects difficult judgments, their causes, common errors, and useful strategies to design and evaluation. Its decision requirements table is one way of representing the findings; [[problem-detection|Problem detection]] illustrates a challenge the analysis may uncover.

## Organizational choice

- [[fact-value-distinction|Fact–value distinction]] — separating empirical premises from preferred ends in Simon's framework.
- [[decision-premises|Decision premises]] — how information and commitments structure a choice.
- [[organizational-authority|Organizational authority]] — accepting another's decision as a premise, with Simon's qualifications concerning expertise and persuasion.

Suggested order: read bounded rationality before satisficing, because Simon's commentary presents satisficing and a simplified world model as the two features of bounded rationality. Read the fact–value distinction before decision premises, and decision premises before organizational authority. When comparing these entries with [[agent|Agent]], [[policy|Policy]], or [[context-window|Context window]], ask what question each definition answers. A model-input limit, for example, is narrower than Simon's limits on attention and on the alternatives considered.

Continue to [[agent-foundations|Agent foundations]] for perception–action and POMDP formulations, or [[memory-and-information|Memory and information]] for retained and retrieved information. The agent examples are our own illustrative applications. They are not attributed to Simon and do not imply that he wrote about contemporary LLM assistants.

## Representation, plans, and joint work

[[joint-project|Joint project]] adds a route through proposed work, joint construal, and commitments: a response may revise the project rather than simply accept or reject a fixed plan. Pair it with [[decision-premises|Decision premises]] when examining which constraints govern a choice, and with [[organizational-authority|Organizational authority]] when examining whose decisions guide action. For a simulated decision or quoted approval, [[layering-in-language-use|Layering in language use]] helps identify the domain and role to which the utterance belongs.

Read [[problem-representation|Problem representation]] for how representations structure operations and organization. [[organizational-communication|Organizational communication]] connects reporting, filtering, and information location to decision premises. For plans in practice, distributed work, function allocation, and monitoring, continue to [[collaboration-across-people-and-artefacts|Collaboration across people and artefacts]].

[[multiverse-analysis|Multiverse analysis]] examines a different decision space: reasonable ways to construct analysis datasets from the same observations. It runs valid alternatives and shows which preprocessing choices change the statistical results.

[[articulation-work|Articulation work]] addresses the ongoing work of making divided contributions fit together, including revising coordination arrangements.
