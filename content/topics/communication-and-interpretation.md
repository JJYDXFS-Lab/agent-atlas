How do participants interpret an utterance, coordinate understanding, and reason about another's perspective?

- [[pragmatics|Pragmatics]] — language use and meaning in context.
- [[common-ground|Common ground]] — information participants take to be shared.
- [[conversational-grounding|Conversational grounding]] — establishing sufficient mutual understanding.
- [[least-collaborative-effort|Least collaborative effort]] — the combined work of establishing understanding.
- [[medium-constraints-on-grounding|Medium constraints on grounding]] — available techniques, costs, and purpose-dependent tradeoffs.
- [[theory-of-mind|Theory of mind]] — attributing and reasoning about mental states.

These concepts overlap without being interchangeable. A system's [[context-window|Context window]] and [[agent-memory|memory]] describe information mechanisms; they do not by themselves establish shared understanding.

## Purpose, medium, and the cost of understanding

Start with [[common-ground|Common ground]] for the shared basis of communication, then [[conversational-grounding|Conversational grounding]] for how participants establish sufficient understanding. Clark and Brennan organize grounding around two interacting factors: what participants are trying to accomplish and which techniques the medium makes available at what cost (pp. 128, 140, 147).[^1]

Continue to [[least-collaborative-effort|Least collaborative effort]] for why a brief repair or an additional exchange may save joint work, then [[medium-constraints-on-grounding|Medium constraints on grounding]] for the eight constraints, eleven interdependent costs, and their interaction with purpose. The latter distinguishes the original human communication framework from Atlas applications to chat, streaming, and background agents.

[^1]: Clark, Herbert H., and Susan E. Brennan. “Grounding in Communication.” 1991. In _Perspectives on Socially Shared Cognition_, 127–149. Especially pp. 140–147 on media constraints, costs, and their interaction with purpose.
