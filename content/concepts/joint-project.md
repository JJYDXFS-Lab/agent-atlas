# Joint project

A server asks for an order; the customer first asks which soup is available. Answering that question lets them return to placing the order. Clark uses this kind of exchange to show how a small piece of joint business can contain another needed to carry it out. (pp. 206–207)[^1]

A **joint project**, in Clark's account, is a joint action proposed by one participant and taken up by the others. A question and its answer can be a minimal project; extended projects develop through connected exchanges. Understanding a proposal is not enough to carry it out: the respondent may understand perfectly and still decline. (pp. 191, 203–204)[^1]

## Common purpose and commitment

For participants to commit to a joint purpose, they must identify it, be able to do their parts, be willing to do them, and treat these conditions as part of their common ground. A proposal and response can help establish those conditions, but a proposal alone cannot establish the respondent's willingness. (p. 203)[^1]

Clark distinguishes a private commitment, a commitment made in another's presence, a commitment made to another, and a **participatory commitment** to doing one's part in a joint action. In the last case, failure can undermine the partner's activity as well as one's own. Institutional roles can make some conditions already settled: in the book's tightly circumscribed builder–assistant example, a short instruction works against assumed duties, capabilities, and a limited range of orders. (pp. 294–295, 297)[^1] [[organizational-authority|Organizational authority]] addresses the distinct question of how another's decision comes to govern action.

## Joint construal and responses

“Sit here” might be an order, request, offer, or advice. A response such as “No thanks” or “What a good idea” displays how the utterance is being construed and lets the speaker validate or correct that construal. Clark's **joint construal** is what participants settle on as what the speaker is to be taken to mean, not simply an unchanged private intention recovered by the listener. (pp. 212–214, 218–219)[^1]

Responses can complete the project as proposed, modify it to something feasible or acceptable, decline it, or withdraw from considering it. Declining leaves the proposed project incomplete; withdrawal can redirect the conversation to another project. These responses shape how the conversation develops locally. (pp. 203–204, 337)[^1]

For a hypothetical report request, “I can check the totals, but not certify the dataset” offers a modified project. “I understand which dataset you mean, but I cannot check it” can provide evidence of understanding while declining the work. [[conversational-grounding|Conversational grounding]] concerns that evidence; its acceptance phase must not be read as a general definition of all uptake. Understanding, endorsing a claim, authorizing an action, and completing work require separate judgments.

## Embedding, chaining, and presequences

- **Embedding:** a project inside another resolves an obstacle, as the soup question does before the order. (pp. 206–207)[^1]
- **Chaining:** a response completes one project while initiating another. An answer to a question is also an assertion that can receive acknowledgment or evaluation. (pp. 207–208)[^1]
- **Presequences:** a preliminary exchange prepares a larger project, as checking availability can prepare a request. The response may allow a shortcut when the larger purpose is mutually evident; it need not commit participants to every possible subsequent action. (pp. 210, 218, 301)[^1]

These structures are assembled opportunistically, not necessarily specified in advance. A conversation can also leave a project incomplete during a digression and later reinstate attention to its relevant common ground. (pp. 212, 337, 346)[^1]

In a tentative agent-workflow analogy, make the proposed work, any revised interpretation, and the outstanding commitments inspectable before treating a task as finished. [[joint-action#activity-state-and-record|Activity state and record]] helps distinguish this evolving business from a transcript. [[joint-action#levels-of-joint-action|Levels of joint action]] locates proposing and considering without equating them with execution.
