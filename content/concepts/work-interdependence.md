# Work interdependence

Schmidt introduces a **fictional illustration** of moving furniture. Two people can carry chairs separately, but moving a heavy table requires coordinated lifting and carrying. A narrow doorway tightens the constraints. A flexible rolled carpet can pass more easily, while making the partner's movement harder to sense through the object itself. These are explanatory scenarios, not reported field experiments. (Chapter 1, pp. 7–9)[^1]

**Work interdependence** concerns how participants' activities depend on one another through the material, dynamic, and environmental characteristics of a cooperative effort. Schmidt uses these relationships to investigate cooperation without requiring a successful shared understanding as its defining starting point. The relevant scope matters: moving chairs may be loosely coupled within a larger relocation that also includes tightly coupled tasks. (pp. 10–13)[^1]

## What differs between dependencies?

Schmidt identifies characteristics including **degree of coupling, direction of dependence, irreversibility, uncertainty, and temporal characteristics**. They guide comparison of work settings rather than supply a fixed scoring instrument. (p. 12)[^1] The table and carpet examples also raise a question about what workers can perceive of one another's actions; perceptibility is not an additional item in that stated list.

Sharing a scarce resource or an organizational budget does not by itself specify this relationship of interdependence in work. Schmidt distinguishes these cases because their relevant practices and rules differ. (p. 12)[^1] An analysis must identify which activities depend on which others and how, rather than infer cooperation solely from membership in a team.

## Different questions about cooperation

[[articulation-work|Articulation work]] concerns coordinating and integrating the interdependent contributions. [[joint-action|Joint action]] and [[joint-project|Joint project]] develop Clark's account of participants' coordinated contributions and joint business. [[common-ground|Common ground]] addresses what participants take to be shared. A material dependency and a basis for mutual understanding are different objects of explanation; identifying the former does not refute the latter.

[[near-decomposability|Near decomposability]] compares strengths and timescales of interactions among subsystems in Simon's account. It should not be substituted for Schmidt's analysis of particular work dependencies.

## A contemporary question

For a hypothetical agent pipeline, identify whose output another participant needs, how late changes propagate, and which mistakes can still be reversed. This is an editorial application of the questions above, not evidence that a software pipeline has all the properties of human cooperative practice.
