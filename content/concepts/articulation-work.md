# Articulation work

Engineers facing a disordered design project devised a bug report form, a binder, and a spreadsheet schedule to make their contributions fit together. Schmidt recounts this study to show practitioners creating and adapting the means of coordination. The forms were part of the work of organizing a divided effort, not evidence that the division of labor already coordinated itself. (Chapter 1, pp. 15–16)[^1]

**Articulation work**, in Schmidt's use of a term adopted from Strauss and colleagues, is the work of coordinating and integrating the contributions of participants in a cooperative effort. Moving furniture and arranging how the movers' contributions fit together are analytically distinguishable, even when they happen together. This entry follows Schmidt's account; it does not claim an independent reading of Strauss. (pp. 10–13)[^1]

## Making and revising arrangements

A division of labor leaves practical questions: who is ready, which contribution depends on another, and what must change when an arrangement no longer works? In Schmidt's account of shop-floor planning, a demonstrator retained the models underlying an MRP system while allowing operators to override inadequate plans and inspect anticipated consequences for interdependent work. It was a design prototype, not a claim that any worker override improves production. (pp. 15–16)[^1]

Schmidt's later discussion asks how practitioners can modify the protocols organizing their cooperation, including separating their specification from the first-order control of the work. Supporting collaboration and supporting the work of adjusting its arrangements are therefore distinct design concerns. (Chapter 11, p. 318; Chapter 13, pp. 409–413)[^1] The unavailable Part II is not a basis for a complete theory of coordination mechanisms here.

## Boundaries

[[work-interdependence|Work interdependence]] identifies the dependencies to be handled. [[joint-project|Joint project]] examines the business participants propose and take up in Clark's account. [[function-allocation|Function allocation and function congruence]] concerns assignments and capabilities; [[joint-cognitive-system|Joint cognitive system]] foregrounds sustaining control. Articulation work adds the question of how participants establish, adjust, and repair the connections among contributions. It is not a label for every productive action or a synonym for a scheduling algorithm.

## A contemporary question

In a hypothetical human–agent review, someone must resolve an ambiguous handoff, change the order of checks, or arrange a replacement reviewer. These are possible sites of articulation work. Whether making those arrangements editable improves coordination is a question to investigate, not a result established by Schmidt's historical cases.
