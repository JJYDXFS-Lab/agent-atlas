# Contextual Control Model (COCOM)

Hollnagel and Woods present the **Contextual Control Model (COCOM)** as a functional approach to how performance is ordered under changing conditions. **Competence** supplies possible actions, **constructs** selectively represent the situation, and **control** concerns how competence is applied. The account describes performance without requiring a literal sequence of inner processing modules. (Chapter 7, pp. 144–148)[^j]

## Control modes

The model distinguishes scrambled, opportunistic, tactical, and strategic control. These characterize patterns ranging from poorly organized action and response to salient cues to procedure-based performance and planning across a broader horizon. They are regions of performance, not discrete hidden mechanisms inferred directly from a person's or model's self-description. Strategic control can be costly to sustain. (pp. 146–148)[^j]

## ECOM and concurrent goals

The Extended Control Model (ECOM) describes tracking, regulating, monitoring, and targeting at different goal levels and timescales. Concurrent goals do not imply that a human performs all actions simultaneously, and these levels are not a recipe for software worker concurrency. The book qualifies the framework as a minimal account of interacting commitments. (pp. 149–155)[^j]

## Time and control

The temporal discussion separates time spent evaluating events, selecting actions, and performing actions from the time available. Delays can arise at different points, so faster selection need not restore timely control. Evaluation and selection can overlap. The mode curves are theoretical, and Table 8.3's indicative time relations are not a validated numerical classifier. This entry therefore supplies no threshold-to-mode scoring rule. (Chapter 8, pp. 161–175, especially 162, 166–170)[^j]

## Boundaries and application

A COCOM construct need not be a normalized probability distribution like a [[belief-state|Belief state]]. A [[policy|Policy]] is a decision rule under a specified formalism, whereas modes characterize how competence is applied. [[satisficing|Satisficing]] concerns an acceptability criterion; an opportunistic action may not meet it. [[execution-monitoring|Execution monitoring]] offers another, planning-specific distinction among checks.

An Atlas application is to investigate whether a time-critical assistant's failure arose during evidence evaluation, action selection, or external execution. Use observed task evidence to test the interpretation. The framework is not an empirically calibrated LLM instrument, and human fatigue, attention, or pre-attentive timing claims do not automatically describe language models.
